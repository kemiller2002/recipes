Smoked Flanken-Cut Short Ribs

A smoke-then-covered-braise method for thin flanken-cut beef short ribs that makes them tender enough to pull easily from the bone.

Smoked Flanken-Cut Short Ribs

Thin flanken-cut short ribs can dry out if treated like thick English-cut ribs. The reliable route to very tender ribs is to give them smoke first, then finish them tightly covered with a small amount of braising liquid.

Ingredients

  • Flanken-cut beef short ribs
  • Kosher salt, to taste
  • Coarse black pepper, to taste
  • Garlic powder, to taste
  • Paprika, to taste
  • 1/2 to 1 cup beef stock
  • Worcestershire sauce, to taste
  • Sliced onion, optional
  • Garlic cloves, optional
  • Barbecue sauce, optional for finishing

Instructions

  1. Season the ribs with salt, black pepper, garlic powder, and paprika.
  2. Heat the smoker to 250°F.
  3. Smoke the ribs uncovered for 60 to 90 minutes.
  4. Transfer the ribs to a covered pan.
  5. Add 1/2 to 1 cup beef stock, a splash of Worcestershire, and optional onion or garlic.
  6. Cover the pan tightly.
  7. Raise the cooking temperature to 275°F.
  8. Cook covered for 2 to 3 hours. Begin checking around the 2-hour mark.
  9. The ribs are ready when a probe or fork enters with almost no resistance and the bones twist or pull with very little resistance. Internal temperature is often 195 to 205°F, but tenderness is the real test.
  10. Uncover, brush with barbecue sauce if desired, and return to the smoker for 10 to 15 minutes to set the exterior.

Timing Target

A useful baseline is about 1 hour of smoke followed by about 2 1/2 hours covered.
