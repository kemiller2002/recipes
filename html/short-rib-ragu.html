Red Wine Braised Short Rib Ragù

Yield: 6–8 servings Time: 4–4½ hours Equipment: Dutch oven

Red Wine Braised Short Rib Ragù

Yield: 6–8 servings Time: 4–4½ hours Equipment: Dutch oven

A rich, restaurant-style short rib ragù with deeply browned beef, red wine, San Marzano tomatoes, and a glossy finish. Serve with pappardelle or creamy Parmesan polenta.

Ingredients

Beef

  • 3–4 lb bone-in beef short ribs (or 2½–3 lb boneless)
  • 2 tsp kosher salt, plus more to taste
  • 1 tsp freshly ground black pepper
  • 2 tbsp olive oil

Aromatics

  • 1 large yellow onion, finely diced
  • 2 carrots, finely diced
  • 2 celery stalks, finely diced
  • 6 garlic cloves, minced

Sauce

  • 2 tbsp tomato paste
  • 1½ cups dry red wine (Chianti, Cabernet, or Sangiovese)
  • 1 cup low-sodium beef stock
  • 1 (28 oz) can whole San Marzano tomatoes, crushed
  • 1 tbsp Worcestershire sauce
  • 2 anchovy fillets or 1 tsp anchovy paste (optional)

Herbs

  • 2 sprigs fresh rosemary
  • 4 sprigs fresh thyme
  • 2 bay leaves
  • 1 Parmesan rind (optional)

To finish

  • 1–2 tsp red wine vinegar
  • 2 tbsp cold unsalted butter
  • 1½ lb pappardelle or rigatoni
  • Freshly grated Parmigiano-Reggiano
  • Chopped fresh parsley

Instructions

  1. Sear: Preheat oven to 300°F. Pat ribs dry and season with salt and pepper. Heat olive oil in a Dutch oven over medium-high heat. Sear ribs in batches until deeply browned on all sides, about 10–15 minutes per batch. Remove.
  2. Soffritto: Reduce heat to medium, leaving about 2 tbsp rendered fat in the pot. Cook onion, carrots, and celery 10–12 minutes until softened and lightly caramelized. Add garlic and optional anchovy; cook 1 minute. Stir in tomato paste and cook 3–4 minutes until brick-red.
  3. Deglaze: Add wine, scraping browned bits from the bottom. Simmer 6–8 minutes until reduced by roughly half. Add stock, tomatoes, Worcestershire, rosemary, thyme, bay leaves, and Parmesan rind.
  4. Braise: Return ribs and juices to the pot. Liquid should reach about two-thirds up the meat; add a splash of stock if necessary. Bring to a gentle simmer, cover, and braise in oven 3–3½ hours until fork-tender.
  5. Finish ragù: Remove ribs, bones, herbs, bay leaves, and rind. Shred meat, discarding excess fat and tough tissue. Skim fat from sauce; simmer uncovered 15–25 minutes to concentrate. Return meat, stir in vinegar and cold butter, and season to taste.
  6. Pasta: Cook pasta 1–2 minutes shy of al dente; reserve 1½ cups pasta water. Toss pasta with ragù and enough pasta water to make a glossy coating. Finish with Parmesan and parsley.

Chef's Upgrades

  • Porcini depth: Soak ½ oz dried porcini in hot water, finely chop, and add with garlic. Strain the soaking liquid and replace some stock with it.
  • Silky texture: For boneless ribs and boxed stock, bloom 1 tsp unflavored gelatin in the cool stock before adding.
  • Brightness: Add a little finely grated lemon zest just before serving.
  • Make ahead: Refrigerate overnight, lift off solidified fat, and reheat gently. The flavor improves.

Notes

  • Thick English-cut short ribs are ideal. Thin flanken-cut ribs may need less braising time; check tenderness earlier.
  • The ragù also works over creamy Parmesan polenta.
  • Sauce should be beef-forward, with tomato supporting rather than dominating.
