Maryland Pit Beef with Professional Rub and Tiger Sauce

Maryland Pit Beef with Professional Rub and Tiger Sauce

The beef is seasoned in two stages, cooked indirectly until rare, then seared hard before resting and slicing paper-thin.

Beef

  • 6 lb top round, divided into manageable roasts if needed

Professional Rub for 6 lb

  • 1 tbsp + 2 tsp Diamond Crystal kosher salt
  • 1 tbsp + 1 tsp coarse black pepper
  • 2 tsp sweet paprika
  • 1 tsp smoked paprika
  • 2 tsp garlic powder
  • 1 1/2 tsp onion powder
  • 3/4 tsp dry mustard
  • 1/2 tsp celery seed
  • 1/2 tsp MSG
  • 1/4 tsp cayenne

If using Morton kosher salt, use about 1 tbsp rather than the larger Diamond Crystal volume.

Tiger Sauce

  • 1 cup mayonnaise
  • 1/3 cup prepared horseradish
  • 1 1/2 tbsp fresh lemon juice
  • 1 small garlic clove, finely grated
  • 1 tsp Dijon mustard
  • 1/2 tsp Worcestershire sauce
  • 1/2 tsp kosher salt, then adjust to taste
  • 1/2 tsp freshly ground black pepper
  • 1/8 tsp cayenne, optional

For Serving

  • Soft Kaiser rolls
  • Thinly sliced raw white onion
  • Dill pickle chips, optional

Instructions

1. Season Ahead

  1. Mix the rub thoroughly.
  2. Apply about 55 to 60 percent of the rub to the beef.
  3. Wrap tightly and refrigerate 6 to 24 hours.

2. Make the Tiger Sauce

  1. Whisk all tiger sauce ingredients until smooth.
  2. Refrigerate at least 1 hour, preferably 4 to 12 hours.

3. Indirect Cook

  1. Prepare a grill for two-zone cooking.
  2. Unwrap the beef and cook on the indirect side until the center reaches about 100°F, usually 45 to 60 minutes depending on roast shape and grill temperature.

4. Re-Season and Sear

  1. Apply the remaining 40 to 45 percent of the rub.
  2. Move the beef over high direct heat.
  3. Sear aggressively, turning as needed, until deeply browned and the center reaches 120 to 125°F, usually another 10 to 20 minutes.

5. Rest and Slice

  1. Rest 15 minutes.
  2. Slice as thinly as possible against the grain.
  3. Serve on Kaiser rolls with raw onion and tiger sauce.

Notes

The barbecue sauces in maryland-pit-beef-bbq.md are optional accompaniments. Tiger sauce remains the classic primary sauce.
