Custard-Rich Hot Chocolate for Eight

A reconstructed large-batch hot chocolate enriched with tempered eggs. It is silkier and fuller-bodied than standard cocoa, but still pourable.

Ingredients

  • 8 cups whole milk
  • or 6 cups whole milk plus 2 cups half-and-half
  • 3/4 cup unsweetened cocoa powder
  • 3/4 cup granulated sugar
  • 1/2 tsp kosher salt
  • 8 oz dark chocolate, 60 to 70%, finely chopped
  • 4 large eggs
  • 1 tsp vanilla extract

Equipment

  • Large heavy saucepan
  • Large mixing bowl
  • Whisk
  • Instant-read thermometer
  • Fine-mesh strainer, recommended

Steps

  1. In a large saucepan, whisk together the cocoa powder, sugar, salt, and 1 cup of the cold milk until completely smooth.
  2. Whisk in the remaining milk.
  3. Heat over medium-low heat, whisking occasionally, until steaming and about 170°F. Do not boil.
  4. Add the chopped dark chocolate and whisk until completely melted.
  5. In a large bowl, whisk the eggs until smooth.
  6. While whisking constantly, slowly ladle about 2 cups of the hot chocolate mixture into the eggs.
  7. Reduce the heat under the saucepan to low.
  8. Slowly whisk the tempered egg mixture back into the saucepan.
  9. Cook gently, stirring or whisking continuously, until the hot chocolate reaches 160 to 165°F and becomes slightly more velvety. Do not allow it to simmer or boil.
  10. Remove from heat.
  11. Stir in the vanilla.
  12. Strain through a fine-mesh strainer for the smoothest texture.
  13. Serve immediately.

Texture Target

The finished drink should:

  • Coat the tongue more fully than ordinary cocoa
  • Remain pourable
  • Feel custardy without tasting eggy
  • Have no visible curds or graininess

Adjustments

  • Richer: use the 6 cups milk plus 2 cups half-and-half option.
  • Darker: use 1 cup cocoa powder rather than 3/4 cup.
  • Sweeter: increase sugar to 1 cup.
  • Less sweet: reduce sugar to 2/3 cup and use 70% chocolate.
  • Spiced: steep cinnamon, ancho chile, espresso powder, or orange peel in the milk.

Reheating

Reheat over low heat while stirring constantly. Keep the mixture below a simmer because the eggs can curdle if overheated.

Reconstruction Note

The core quantities were preserved from the earlier recipe. The tempering sequence and temperature controls were rebuilt to make the formula safe, repeatable, and smooth.
