# Competition-Style Texas-Inspired Chili

A hearty Midwestern-meets-Texas chili with deep beef flavor, smoky dried chilies, and a rich sauce. Beans are optional. Designed as a family-dinner recipe with competition-style flavor layering; not an assertion of compliance with any competition's rules.

## Ingredients

### Meat and base
- 2 lb beef chuck roast, cut into ½-inch cubes
- 1 lb ground beef (80/20 preferred)
- 2 tbsp neutral oil
- 2 large yellow onions, diced
- 1 poblano pepper, diced
- 1 red bell pepper, diced
- 6 cloves garlic, minced
- 2 tbsp tomato paste
- 1 can (28 oz) crushed tomatoes
- 2 cups low-sodium beef stock, plus additional liquid for the chili paste as listed below
- 1 tbsp Worcestershire sauce
- 2 cans (15 oz each) kidney or pinto beans, drained (optional)

### Homemade chili paste
- 3 dried ancho chilies
- 2 dried guajillo chilies
- 1 dried pasilla chili
- 1 chipotle pepper in adobo
- 1 cup hot beef stock or water

### Spice blend
- 2 tbsp ground cumin
- 2 tsp smoked paprika
- 2 tsp Mexican oregano
- 1 tsp ground coriander
- ½ tsp cayenne, or to taste
- ½ tsp freshly ground black pepper
- 1½ tsp kosher salt initially, then adjust to taste

### Flavor boosters and finish
- 1 tbsp unsweetened cocoa powder
- 1 tsp instant espresso powder
- 1 tbsp dark brown sugar
- 1 tbsp apple cider vinegar
- 1 tbsp masa harina (optional, for thickening)

## Method

1. **Make the chili paste.** Stem and seed the dried chilies. Toast in a dry skillet 15–20 seconds per side until fragrant, without burning. Soak in 1 cup hot stock or water for 20 minutes, then blend with the chipotle until smooth.
2. **Brown the beef.** Heat oil in a heavy Dutch oven. Brown chuck in batches until deeply caramelized; remove. Brown ground beef in the same pot. Reserve the beef and leave the fond in the pot.
3. **Build the aromatics.** Cook onions and peppers in the beef fat 8–10 minutes. Add garlic for 1 minute. Cook tomato paste 2–3 minutes until darkened. Stir in the spice blend and toast 30 seconds.
4. **Simmer.** Return the beef and juices. Add chili paste, crushed tomatoes, 2 cups stock, Worcestershire, cocoa, espresso, and brown sugar. Simmer partially covered over low heat for 2–2½ hours, stirring occasionally, until the chuck is fork-tender. Add stock or water if the pot becomes too dry.
5. **Finish.** If using beans, add them during the final 30 minutes. Stir in vinegar and adjust salt and acidity. If thicker chili is desired, whisk masa harina with a little cold water, add to the pot, and simmer 10 more minutes.
6. Rest for 20 minutes before serving. Flavor improves overnight.

## Why It Works
- **Ancho** brings dark fruit and sweetness; **guajillo** brightness; **pasilla** earthiness.
- Deep browning develops savory fond and a fuller beef flavor.
- Cocoa and espresso reinforce roasted notes without tasting distinctly like chocolate or coffee.
- Vinegar at the end balances the richness.

## Optional Upgrade
After browning the meat, deglaze with **½ cup malty brown ale** and reduce by half before cooking the vegetables. This adds malt complexity. If using the same pot, temporarily remove the meat and reduce the beer before continuing with the aromatics.

## Serving
Top with sharp cheddar, finely diced white onion, sour cream, and fresh jalapeños. Serve with skillet cornbread.

## Variations
- **Meat-forward:** Omit beans and reduce liquid as needed for a thicker chili.
- **Midwestern family style:** Include both cans of beans and add a little extra stock if desired.
- **Heat:** Adjust cayenne and chipotle to taste.
