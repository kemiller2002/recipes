# Maple Five Ways

A reconstructed plated dessert built around five distinct maple expressions. The original concept and financier quantities were preserved; the remaining ratios have been standardized into a complete working recipe.

The goal is not maximum sweetness. Each component should express maple differently: toasted, custardy, airy, crisp, and warm.

## Snapshot

- Yield: 8 plated desserts
- Prep Time: about 1 hour active
- Cook Time: about 35 minutes
- Chill/Rest Time: at least 4 hours
- Difficulty: Hard
- Best For: competitions, tasting menus, special dinners

## Why This Works

- Brown butter and almond give the financier enough bitterness and toast to support maple.
- The crémeux provides the densest maple expression without becoming frosting-sweet.
- Chantilly adds a cold, airy contrast.
- The tuile supplies height and brittle texture.
- The warm salted glaze reconnects every component at service.

## Brown-Butter Maple Financier

### Yield

8 to 10 portions, depending on mold size

### Ingredients

- 170 g unsalted butter
- 90 g maple sugar
- 60 g all-purpose flour
- 60 g almond flour
- 180 g egg whites
- 30 g pure maple syrup
- 2 g kosher salt

### Steps

1. Heat oven to 375°F.
2. Brown the butter over medium heat until the milk solids are deeply golden and nutty.
3. Strain if desired and cool until warm but still fluid.
4. Whisk together maple sugar, flour, almond flour, and salt.
5. Add the egg whites and maple syrup and whisk just until smooth.
6. Slowly whisk in the brown butter.
7. Rest the batter 20 to 30 minutes if time allows.
8. Portion into buttered financier molds or a shallow baking pan.
9. Bake 14 to 18 minutes, until deeply golden at the edges and springy in the center.
10. Cool before unmolding.
11. For plated service, trim or cut into neat rectangles.

## Maple Crémeux

### Yield

About 2 cups

### Ingredients

- 200 g heavy cream
- 100 g whole milk
- 75 g pure maple syrup
- 25 g maple sugar
- 4 large egg yolks, about 70 to 75 g
- 2.5 g powdered gelatin
- 15 g cold water
- 1 g kosher salt

### Steps

1. Bloom the gelatin in the cold water for 5 to 10 minutes.
2. Heat the cream, milk, maple syrup, maple sugar, and salt until steaming.
3. Whisk the yolks in a heatproof bowl.
4. Slowly whisk about one-third of the hot dairy into the yolks.
5. Return the tempered yolk mixture to the saucepan.
6. Cook over medium-low heat, stirring constantly, until the custard reaches 178 to 180°F and lightly coats a spoon.
7. Remove from heat and whisk in the bloomed gelatin until dissolved.
8. Strain through a fine-mesh sieve.
9. Cover the surface and chill at least 4 hours.
10. Before plating, whisk briefly until smooth and transfer to a piping bag.

## Maple Chantilly

### Yield

About 2 1/2 cups

### Ingredients

- 240 g heavy cream, very cold
- 20 g maple sugar
- 20 g pure maple syrup
- 1 g kosher salt
- 1/2 tsp vanilla extract, optional

### Steps

1. Chill the bowl and whisk if possible.
2. Combine the cream, maple sugar, maple syrup, salt, and optional vanilla.
3. Whip to soft-medium peaks.
4. Refrigerate until service.
5. Rewhisk briefly by hand if it relaxes.

## Maple Tuile

### Yield

8 to 12 tuiles

### Ingredients

- 50 g pure maple syrup
- 30 g unsalted butter, melted
- 30 g granulated sugar
- 30 g all-purpose flour
- 30 g egg white
- Pinch kosher salt

### Steps

1. Heat oven to 325°F.
2. Whisk the maple syrup, melted butter, sugar, flour, egg white, and salt until smooth.
3. Chill the batter 20 to 30 minutes.
4. Spread very thin ovals or strips on a silicone baking mat.
5. Bake 6 to 8 minutes, until bubbling subsides and the edges are deep golden.
6. Working quickly, lift each tuile while hot and drape over a rolling pin or shape into a gentle curve.
7. Cool completely until crisp.
8. Store airtight with a desiccant packet if humidity is high.

## Warm Salted Maple Glaze

### Yield

About 3/4 cup

### Ingredients

- 120 g pure maple syrup
- 30 g unsalted butter
- 1 g kosher salt

### Steps

1. Warm the maple syrup gently until slightly reduced and glossy, 2 to 3 minutes.
2. Remove from heat.
3. Whisk in the cold butter a little at a time.
4. Add the salt.
5. Hold warm, not hot, for service.

## Assembly

For each plate:

1. Place one financier slightly off center.
2. Pipe 2 or 3 neat dots or a short line of maple crémeux beside it.
3. Add a quenelle or spoonful of maple Chantilly.
4. Stand one maple tuile vertically to create height.
5. Spoon a small amount of warm salted maple glaze around the financier, not over the tuile.
6. Serve immediately.

## Optional Competition Finish

Use only if the plate needs contrast:

- Toasted chopped pecans
- A few grains of flaky salt
- Very small amount of tart apple brunoise

Avoid adding another dominant flavor. The plate should still read unmistakably as maple.

## Make-Ahead

- Financier: 1 day ahead
- Crémeux: 2 days ahead
- Chantilly: same day
- Tuile: 1 day ahead, airtight
- Glaze: same day; rewarm gently
